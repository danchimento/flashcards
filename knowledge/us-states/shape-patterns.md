# U.S. States — Shape Patterns

Shape "families" that make good themed levels or teaching hooks. (Per-state
mnemonics live in `src/content/usStateHints.js`.)

## Rectangle / near-rectangle states
The two true rectangles plus the blocky Plains states — easy to draw, easy to
confuse with each other, which is the fun.

- True rectangles: **Colorado, Wyoming**
- Near-rectangles: Kansas, Nebraska (with a notch), North Dakota, South Dakota,
  New Mexico, Utah (with a bite out of the NE corner)

## Panhandle states
A narrow strip projecting from the main body.
[Source](https://www.worldatlas.com/articles/panhandle-states.html)

- Alaska, Connecticut, Florida, Idaho, Maryland, Nebraska, Oklahoma, Texas,
  West Virginia (two panhandles)

## "Twin" / easily-confused pairs
Great for a dedicated "tell them apart" level.

- North Dakota ↔ South Dakota (near-identical outline)
- North Carolina ↔ South Carolina
- New Hampshire ↔ Vermont (mirror triangles; VT is landlocked, NH has a seacoast)
- Kansas ↔ Nebraska (blocky Plains)
- Mississippi ↔ Alabama (side-by-side rectangles on the Gulf)

## Famous-outline states (great Tier-1 anchors)
- Michigan — the mitten (Lower Peninsula)
- Louisiana — the boot
- Oklahoma — the frying pan / cleaver
- Florida — the peninsula + panhandle
- Texas — panhandle "chimney" + Rio Grande curve
- California — the long Pacific edge
- Idaho — a logger's chair / lowercase "i"

## Multi-state landmarks
- **Four Corners**: Colorado, Utah, Arizona, New Mexico (four states meet at a point)
- **MIMAL** figure down the Mississippi: Minnesota, Iowa, Missouri, Arkansas, Louisiana
- **Great Lakes** ring: Minnesota, Wisconsin, Illinois, Indiana, Michigan, Ohio,
  Pennsylvania, New York
