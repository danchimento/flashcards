# U.S. States — Difficulty Tiers

How hard each state is to identify/place on a blank map, for ordering levels
(easy → hard) and deciding when to ramp modality. Based on recognizability
surveys and shape distinctiveness.

Sources: [Movoto "where are the 50 states" study](https://www.movoto.com/blog/heres-what-happens-when-you-ask-400-people-where-all-50-states-are/),
[commonly-missed states](https://www.jetpunk.com/quizzes/map-quiz-us-states).

## Tier 1 — Iconic / near-universal (start here)
Almost everyone can identify these; big and/or unmistakable shapes.

- California, Texas, Florida, Alaska, Hawaii
- Distinctive shapes: Michigan (mitten), Louisiana (boot), Oklahoma (pan)

## Tier 2 — Recognizable
Distinct position or shape; most people get them with a little thought.

- Pacific/Mountain: Washington, Oregon, Nevada, Arizona, Utah, Colorado, Idaho, Montana
- Large/edge: New York, Minnesota, Wisconsin, Maine
- Named-river/border anchors: Mississippi River line states

## Tier 3 — Trickier
Interior states without a coastline anchor; commonly mixed up.

- Great Plains rectangles: Kansas, Nebraska, Iowa, Wyoming
- Midwest interior: Illinois, Indiana, Ohio (~43% can place Ohio), Missouri
- Upper south: Kentucky, Tennessee, Arkansas, West Virginia
- Mid-South: Alabama, Mississippi (~52%), Georgia, South/North Carolina

## Tier 4 — Hardest (save for last)
Small, clustered, or near-identical shapes — "utter pandemonium" in surveys.

- The Dakotas: North Dakota & South Dakota (identical outline, only N/S differs)
- New England small states: Rhode Island, Connecticut, Massachusetts, New Hampshire, Vermont
- Mid-Atlantic small states: Delaware, New Jersey, Maryland

## Practical ordering for levels
1. Iconic anchors (Tier 1) →
2. West by region (Pacific, Four Corners, Rockies) →
3. Great Plains + Midwest interior (Tiers 2–3) →
4. The South (Tier 3) →
5. Mid-Atlantic →
6. **New England last** (Tier 4) →
7. Cumulative mastery of all 50.
